Everything I worked on lives behind an NDA, so the hardware stays off the internet. There were lasers, custom jigs, and a small robot fixing a supplier’s mistakes. I’m happy to talk through any of it in person.

What I worked on
01Accelerated test jig

Shaking it until it breaks

A fastening mechanism on one of our production tools kept failing every so often, and every failure took the equipment down with it. Waiting around for it to fail on its own wasn’t an option, so I built a test jig that shook the part at the same vibration frequency the equipment actually sees, squeezing months of wear into a much shorter test. Once we knew how it failed, we could fix it before it stopped the line again.

~10% availability
02Autonomous production tool

A robot for someone else’s mistake

A supplier process was sending us current collectors that weren’t always lined up, and that one issue was the single biggest availability and scrap loss on the floor. Instead of waiting on the supplier, I designed a small autonomous tool with timed actuation that pushes misaligned parts back into place right on the line. It went from a sketch to running in production.

Largest floor loss, gone
03Root cause analysis

Chasing a yield leak in laser welding

Cell laser welding was losing a lot of yield, and nobody had a clean answer for why. I owned the root-cause analysis, ran DOEs, and dug through the data in JMP until the pattern finally showed up, then pushed the process changes through to production. Downstream, that came out to roughly ten thousand more good cells every week.

+~10k cells / week
04Equipment upgrade

Cleaner cells, less downtime

One piece of equipment was letting contamination into cells. I designed an upgrade that cut that contamination sharply and, almost as a bonus, made the tool easier to maintain. It was one piece of the push to raise cell output for Cybercab.

−25% maintenance downtime
05Rapid prototyping

A cheap shield for an expensive failure

A recurring failure on production equipment had a simple cause and an expensive outcome. I prototyped a protective barrier, ran it through production trials, and rolled it out, saving about $1,000 every time that failure would have happened.

> 2.5% availability
06Fleetwide standardization

Getting every welder to agree

Laser welders across the fleet weren’t all running at the same power, and it showed up as uneven weld strength. I led the effort to standardize weld power and recalibrated each tool myself, which made the whole fleet noticeably more consistent.

−20% weld asymmetry
